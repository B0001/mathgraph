## Hedges I was tempted to soften — and didn't

`README.md:16` and `README.md:280-281` state "best achievable precision is
~67%, on 3 answers out of 349." `349 = 175(old present-arm n) + 174
(absent-arm n)`. Since the present arm is confirmed to have moved 175→176
everywhere else, the arithmetic temptation was to just change "349" to "350"
inline. **I did not do this** — the "349" denominator being wrong doesn't
tell you whether "67%" or "3 answers" (the numerator) are still right; that
requires actually re-running a combined-arm precision sweep, which does not
exist as a reproducible command anywhere in this codebase (confirmed by
`grep -n "sweep\|calibrat" mathgraph/cli.py` and reading `evaluate.py`'s
`sweep`/`sweep_zero_false`/`graph_arms`, all of which operate on the
different 439-pair non-PFR blueprint corpus, not PFR present+absent
combined). Filed as `mathgraph-aec` instead of guessing. This is the single
most load-bearing negative number in the file (first paragraph, directly
supports "neither benchmark admits a trustworthy operating point") — it gets
a bead with an explicit instruction not to soften the conclusion when it's
recomputed, only to report whatever the new number is.

Same reasoning applied to `README.md:1029-1039` (verify-layer 5-population
table, `mathgraph-2zj`) and `README.md:1105-1106` (bench_release description,
`mathgraph-036`): both cite 175/174 and are plausibly stale by the same
scanner-fix mechanism, but neither has a live reproducing command in this
checkout, so I filed beads describing exactly what's missing rather than
inventing a "current" number I can't actually produce.
